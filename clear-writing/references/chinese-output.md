# Chinese output

Use neutral Mainland Simplified Chinese when Chinese is requested without a regional variant. Preserve or follow Traditional Chinese, Taiwan, or Hong Kong conventions when the user explicitly requests them or asks to revise text already written in that variant.

Compose directly in Chinese. Do not preserve English syntax and replace only the words.

## Sentence construction

- Prefer a clear actor and action when either could be ambiguous.
- Use strong, specific verbs instead of noun-heavy phrases built around generic actions such as conduct, perform, realize, demonstrate, or embody.
- Reduce long pre-nominal modifier chains. Split the thought or state the main action earlier.
- Remove repeated possessive particles and redundant subjects when Chinese syntax makes the reference clear.
- Avoid unnecessary passive constructions and abstract subjects copied from English.
- Let sentence length and structure vary with the information. Do not impose numerical length targets.

## Flow and terminology

- Use connectors only when the relationship is not already evident.
- Keep necessary technical terms, symbols, Latin abbreviations, names, and project vocabulary stable.
- Prefer terminology already used by the user or project over a newly coined translation.
- Preserve the requested level of formality. Professional Chinese should remain professional without becoming bureaucratic or literary.

## Patterns to repair

- Translation-shaped word order and abstract framing.
- Empty action phrases and excessive nominalization.
- Long chains of modifiers or repeated possessive particles.
- Mechanical parallelism, forced contrasts, and ceremonial conclusions.
- Stock openings, generic significance claims, and unsupported evaluative adjectives.
- Sentences whose individual words are familiar but whose combined meaning is unnatural or unclear.

Do not force colloquial language, idioms, rhetorical flourishes, or sentence fragmentation. The target is natural Chinese for the actual context.
