# English output

Use direct, specific, ordinary English that fits the reader and genre.

## Sentence construction

- Prefer a visible subject and a concrete action.
- Use simple copular verbs when they are the clearest choice.
- Keep necessary technical vocabulary. Define it instead of replacing it with a vague everyday word.
- Vary sentence length because the ideas differ, not to simulate personality.
- Use passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or less important than the action.
- Use punctuation according to meaning. Do not ban dashes, semicolons, parentheses, or contractions mechanically.

## Structure and movement

- Begin with the answer when the answer is ready.
- Make causal, conditional, and contrast relationships explicit when the reader needs them.
- Prefer paragraphs for connected reasoning and lists for genuine parallel items.
- End when the information is complete. Do not append a generic conclusion.

## Patterns to repair

- Inflated vocabulary where a common precise word works.
- Generic framing, announcements, and signposting before the content.
- Claims of importance without a comparison or consequence.
- Vague attributions such as unnamed experts or reports.
- Formulaic contrasts, repeated restatements, and manufactured punchlines.
- Promotional adjectives and abstract nouns that hide the underlying fact.
- A technically grammatical sentence whose purpose remains unclear.

## Voice

Match an available user sample or established project voice when relevant. Otherwise, choose a natural register for the task. Keep neutral technical prose neutral. Add first person, humor, informality, or emotion only when the authorial context supports it.
